06Valid, reliable, fair, authentic and secure assessment

Illustrative evidenceAssessment strategy, blueprints, task instructions and authenticity controls.

07Consistent marking and moderation

Support comparable assessment decisions across assessors, sites and delivery modes.

Illustrative evidenceMarking rubrics, assessor guidance and moderation records.

08Competent faculty and adequate resources

Demonstrate appropriate teaching and assessment capability, materials and infrastructure.

Illustrative evidenceFaculty profiles, assessor competence and resource availability.

09Program governance, review and change control

Control program approval, periodic review and material changes.

Illustrative evidenceApproval records, review minutes and a maintained change log.

10Outcomes, controlled certification and accurate claims

Monitor results and ensure award issuance and public statements remain accurate.

Illustrative evidenceOutcome analysis, certification controls and approved publicity.

Assessment expectations

Evidence that matches
the outcome you promise.

The intended complexity of each learning outcome should be reflected in the assessment used to establish it.

Connect every learning outcome to an appropriate assessment, clear criteria and trustworthy evidence.

Clear assessment rules

Explain conditions, pass rules, permitted resources, reassessment and appeals before assessment.

Authentic learner work

Use proportionate identity, authorship and disclosure controls for remote or AI-assisted work.

Consistent judgements

Provide marking guidance and moderation that support defensible results across assessors and delivery settings.

Risk-aware sampling

Include sites, modes, result bands, borderline decisions and reassessments in moderation sampling.

Understand the decision

Review outcomes,
clearly explained.

The outcome reflects the evidence available and whether the material criteria have been satisfied.

Select an outcome to understand its meaning. Developmental recognition and deferral should not be presented as full accreditation.

Accredited

All material review criteria are met.

Accredited with conditions

Limited deficiencies can be addressed without unacceptable risk to learners or the credential.

Developmental recognition

The design shows promise, but stronger evidence, pilot outcomes or assessment is needed before full accreditation.

Deferred

Essential evidence, a delivery sample, a pilot or corrective action remains outstanding.

Refused

Material criteria remain unmet, or credibility and learner risk are unacceptable.

Keep the approved program current

Control the changes.

Must we hold institutional accreditation first?

The program framework allows a program to be reviewed independently or together with institutional accreditation. Confirm the applicable route for your program with CISCM.

Can online or blended programs be reviewed?

Classroom, live virtual, online, blended and work-based programs are included in the published program types. Delivery mode forms part of the approved scope.

Does accreditation apply to every version of our program?

The decision is limited to the approved specification and scope, including title, version and delivery arrangements. Material changes normally require prior approval.

How should AI-assisted assessment be managed?

Remote or AI-assisted work requires proportionate identity, authorship and disclosure controls. Assessment rules should make the permitted resources and conditions clear.

Is developmental recognition the same as full accreditation?

No. It indicates a promising design requiring stronger evidence, pilot outcomes or assessment before full accreditation.

What are the fees, timeframe and validity period?

Confirm these details directly with CISCM for the specific program and scope. This design does not assume a fee, review turnaround or approval duration.

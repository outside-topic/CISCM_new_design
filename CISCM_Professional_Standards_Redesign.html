A common language of competence

From knowing a topic
to demonstrating capability.

The Body of Knowledge defines the assessable scope for CISCM certification and supports capability mapping across membership, professional development and workplace roles.

The framework defines the capability and evidence expected. Professionals may develop through suitable learning and experience routes; it is not a mandatory training course.

A cumulative scale

Five levels.
Increasing depth of responsibility.

Each proficiency level assumes command of those below it. Select a level to explore the capability expected.

Level 1 · Aware

Recognize core concepts, terminology and purpose, and know when specialist help is needed.

Level 2 · Applied

Use established procedures and defined methods to produce dependable results in normal conditions.

Level 3 · Analytical

Diagnose problems, select methods, interpret ambiguity and explain a recommendation.

Level 4 · Architectural

Design systems, processes or policies and defend trade-offs across cost, service, risk and stakeholder needs.

Level 5 · Authoritative

Set professional direction under uncertainty, establish practice others follow and carry enterprise accountability.

The expected level depends on the competency unit, role or credential. A higher level reflects a different scope of judgement and accountability.

Level 3 / Analytical

Investigate the presented problem and develop a reasoned recommendation.

Level 4 / Architectural

Question the problem definition, design the system that should exist and defend competing objectives. This distinction underpins the Chartered tier.

Governance and review

The framework is owned by the Standards and Qualifications Committee and approved by the CISCM Board.

2029

Scheduled full review of the Second Edition.

Interim technical amendments may address material change or corrections. They should carry effective dates and should not apply retrospectively to candidates already assessed.

Open use with attribution. Employers, members, candidates and recognized providers may cite and map against the framework. It may not form the reproduced basis of a competing credential.
